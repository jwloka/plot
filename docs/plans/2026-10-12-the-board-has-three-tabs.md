# The board has three tabs

> The board's tabs Stories, Plans and Agents become Discovery, Planning and Delivery, the old `?tab=` values redirect, the Planning tab shows plans from Design to Testing with a *Show released* checkbox, and the Delivery tab selects the Active sprints in one step, shows a failed verification in *Waiting on you*, and filters its tickets by assignee on the server.

- **Type:** feature

- **Review:** pr
- **Rounds:** 3
- **Impl:** own branches

## Changelog

- The board's tabs are Discovery, Planning and Delivery. `?tab=stories`, `?tab=plans`, `?tab=board` and `?tab=agents` redirect to the new tab, so existing bookmarks keep working.
- The Planning tab shows plans in Design, Development and Testing. A *Show released* checkbox, off by default, adds Released plans.
- An *Active sprint* button in the Delivery tab's sprint filter selects every Active sprint (D32).
- A failed or stopped verification run shows in *Waiting on you* with its reason, its log and its restart command, and a fleet that the 3-failure guard halted says so there.
- The Delivery tab's *Waiting on you* shows only the tickets assigned to you by default, and says how many open tickets it hides, counted over every open ticket the board reads and not only the 50 it sends, with a control that shows every ticket. A GitHub reader with no `People` entry is matched by login (D33). A reader with no known login, or a source that states no assignee, sees every ticket and a sentence that names the reason. On Jira a reader whose `People` entry names no `jira:` account sees the tickets Jira assigns to them, as today; a reader whose `jira:` spelling names none of those tickets sees them too, with a sentence that names the spelling Jira returns.
- *Only my work* also filters tickets: it hides a ticket assigned to somebody else. It filtered PRs by author and plans by `Assignee:` before this plan, and it still shows a branch without a PR and a build, because neither names an owner (D31).

## Design

### Approach

The design spec is [DESIGN-phases.md](../stories/plot-works-in-five-phases/DESIGN-phases.md) §5.

**The tab is a domain answer.** A new rule `boardTabOf(param)` in `packages/domain/src/rules/board-tab.ts` returns the tab and, for an old value, the new value to redirect to: `stories` to `discovery`, `plans` and `board` to `planning`, `agents` to `delivery`. An absent or unknown value answers `planning`, as `plans` is the default today. `App.tsx` calls it in place of `parseTab` and replaces the URL silently, as `parseTab` does today. Every `App.tsx` line the grep above names takes the new value, and `revealBranch` (`:683-685`) writes `?tab=delivery`. The tab names are fixed (D6). The Discovery tab renders today's `StoriesTab` until `tickets-and-stories-have-lanes` rebuilds it; that plan owns the empty-Stories send to Plans at `App.tsx:989-991`.

**The Planning tab.** `PhaseColumn` gains `releasedOnly: boolean`, true for a phase whose only plan state is `released`; `phaseColumns` sets it beside `checklist`. The payload changes: `ColumnSchema` gains `releasedOnly: z.boolean().default(false)`, and `board.ts:2336` passes it through the existing spread. `planningColumns(columns, showReleased)` in `workflow-phases.ts` takes any list of `{ releasedOnly?: boolean }` and drops a column whose `releasedOnly` is true unless `showReleased` is on; the client calls it over the payload's `board.columns`, because *Show released* is per viewer and Released plans already ride the payload. A payload from an older server has no flag, and the client casts and does not parse it, so `planningColumns` reads an absent flag as false and draws Released as today. With the default map it answers Design, Development and Testing, and Released with the checkbox on. A custom map (D12, D26) that puts `plan:released` in a phase with another plan state keeps that column whole, so the checkbox never hides a Delivered plan. The sprint filter, the story filter, *Story lanes* and *Only my work* stay. *Show released* is off by default and is kept in the URL (`?released=1`), as `DESIGN-phases.md` §5 states. `?released=1` belongs to the Planning tab alone: the Discovery tab's toggle uses `?archived=1`, because `App.tsx:633-638` keeps every parameter on a tab switch.

**Active sprint (D32).** A button over the existing multiselect, not a second rule. `fleet.sprints` holds only Active sprints, so "the active sprint's rows" is the multiselect with every option selected, and `slugPassesSprintFilter` passes a row in any selected sprint: the union story Open Point 12 asks for. `activeSprintButton(sprints)` beside `sanitizeSelection` in `packages/board/src/app/lib/filters.ts` answers `{ selection, disabled, sentence }`: every slug of `fleet.sprints`, and `disabled` with *No sprint is Active.* for none. The button sets the selection through the existing `setSprintFilter` (`AgentList.tsx:527`); the rows, the exempt mark and the hidden count come from the existing join. Moving that join into the domain changes no behaviour and is outside this plan.

**The verification row.** It follows the Draft-plan path, because a failed verification is a plan-level row of the same shape: a plan, a sentence, no branch. `verificationRows(verification)` in `packages/board/src/app/lib/agent-rows/verification-row.ts` answers one entry per `fleet.verification.rows` row with its plan, `reason`, `log`, `restart` command (`/plot-deliver --verify <slug>`) and `stopped` mark, plus one halted line when `verification.halted` is true: the fleet starts no verify run until a person restarts one. `VerificationRowView` renders an entry through `tupleFromPlan` and `TupleRowView`, as `DraftPlanRowView` does. `AgentList.tsx:1123` adds the entries beside `drafts` for the `waiting-on-you` key, and the count at `:1090` adds them. No `AgentRow` kind and no server sort change. The rows come from `verificationRow` (`packages/domain/src/rules/auto-verify.ts:321`, `(slug, commit, store, log, now)`), which fleetd writes to `.plot/state/auto-verify-in-flight.json`; the board decides nothing about them. The entry shape names no verify field beyond `{ plan, reason, log, restart, stopped }`, so `the-fleet-writes-the-design` reuses `VerificationRowView` for its failed spec-run row. The slice closes #1640's *The board row* item (H7).

**The assignee.** `Issue` gains `assignees: string[]`, the tracker's own logins or accounts, `[]` where the tracker states none. A list, because a GitHub issue carries several assignees; a ticket is the reader's when any element resolves to the reader. It is a read, under the reasoning that admitted `status` and `statusCategory` (`issue.ts:25-31`): Plot reads it and never writes it, and the status write's refusal to widen (`ports/tracker.ts:31-41`) does not change. The `plot-host.sh issue-list` arms read it: the GitHub arm adds `assignees` to its `--json` list (`:4732`) and `[.assignees[].login]` to its `jq` projection (`:4734-4736`); the Jira arm adds `assignee` to `fields=` (`:4696`) and projects its `emailAddress`, or its `accountId` where the instance hides the email. The Bitbucket arm is unchanged: `bb issue list` prints no assignee (`plot-host.sh:2132`). Each key goes on an existing line, so the slice adds no net shell lines (`scripts/check-shell-lines.sh pr`).

**The assignee's path to the board.** The slice waits on the spike `feature/the-fleet-indexes-issues` of `tickets-and-stories-have-lanes`, which creates `IssueIndexSchema`. After the spike, the board reads the inbox from `issues.json` under a fleet, which the fold writes through `trackerReads`/`issueOf` (`adapters/tracker/tracker-shell.ts:46`, `:120`), and with no fleet through `refreshIssues`. The slice extends `issueOf`, `IssueIndexSchema`, the board's index-row projection, `refreshIssues`' projection (`fleet.ts:1536-1561`) and `IssueRowSchema` with its doc. `IssueIndexSchema` decodes an absent `assignees` as `[]` and `ISSUE_INDEX_VERSION` is not raised, so no upgrade shows *the fleet has left no issue index to read* for up to 22 minutes. An index row with no `assignees` reads as unassigned; nothing decides on the field until `feature/the-delivery-tab-filters-by-sprint-and-owner`, the next wave, which also waits on a later lanes slice, by which time each fleet pass has rewritten the index.

**The ticket owner.** `OwnedRow` gains `{ kind: 'ticket'; assignees: string[]; source: TicketSource }`, and `ownership` answers it through `ticketOwnership`. `TicketSource` is `'github' | 'bitbucket' | 'jira'`, and `ticketSourceOf(source, gitHost)` derives it from the domain's `IssueSource` (`rules/issue-source.ts:27-30`): `tracker` with scheme `jira` is `jira`, `tracker` with scheme `github-issues` (`adapters/tracker/tracker-listers.ts:14-17`) is `github`, and `git-host` takes the git host backend, `github` or `bitbucket`; `nobody` reads no tickets. No parallel resolver exists beside `prOwnership` and `planOwnership`.

- **GitHub and Bitbucket (D33).** Each assignee is a host login, as a PR author is, so the arm compares it to the reader's host login through `resolvePerson` (`entities/person.ts:43`) as `prOwnership` (`ownership.ts:67-73`) does: `mine` when any assignee resolves to the reader, `theirs` when every assignee resolves to another person. An undeclared login resolves to itself, so a GitHub reader owns their own tickets with no `People` entry, as they own their own PRs. The reader is unmatched only with no known login.
- **Jira.** An assignee is a Jira email or account ID, which `People` names as a spelling `jira:<value>` (D21; `parsePersonDirectory` at `person.ts:75` reads it as a spelling of the handle). `mine` when a `jira:` spelling of the reader's handle names any assignee, `theirs` when a `jira:` spelling of another handle names it, `unknown` when no handle declares it, as `planOwnership` (`ownership.ts:83-90`) treats an undeclared spelling.
- **An unassigned ticket.** An empty `assignees` answers `unknown`, as an empty PR author does. `isMine` keeps it, so *Only my work* shows an unassigned ticket and hides only a ticket assigned to somebody else. The inbox default is stricter, because D9 shows "only tickets assigned to the current user": it hides the unassigned ticket and counts it.

**Waiting on you is decided on the server.** The server holds the reader and every open row it read; the client holds 50 rows and no source. So `waitingTickets(rows, inbox, reader, source, complete)` in `packages/domain/src/rules/waiting-tickets.ts` runs in the board's issue read, after the planned filter (`fleet.ts:1579`) and before the cap, and answers `{ tickets, hidden, notice }`. The read builds the `Reader` from the identity and the `People` directory of `server-info.ts:178` and `:205-216`, and the source through `ticketSourceOf`. The rows are every unplanned open row the read holds: the slice waits on `feature/the-index-holds-closed-issues`, from which both the fold and the no-fleet `refreshIssues` read up to `ISSUE_INDEX_OPEN_LIMIT` (1000) open rows with `complete` (`tickets-and-stories-have-lanes.md:73`, `:108`); before it the read holds 50, and no count beyond them is possible. The server then cuts `tickets` to `ISSUE_LIMIT` (50), newest first.

- **The payload.** `FleetShape` gains `waiting: { tickets: IssueRow[]; hidden: number; complete: boolean; notice: string | null; source: TicketSource | null }`, default `null`. `issues` keeps its meaning, the 50 newest unplanned open rows, and is the list *show every ticket* renders. The control is component state, off on each load, as the sprint selection is. The client renders `waiting` and decides nothing; a payload from an older server with no `waiting` renders `issues` as today.
- **A matched reader** sees the tickets whose `ticketOwnership` is `mine`, and `hidden` counts every other unplanned open row, before the cap. On this repository a GitHub reader sees 0 tickets and *127 open tickets are not assigned to you — show every ticket.* With `complete: false` the sentence reads *at least 1000*. So nothing is hidden silently (D22).
- **GitHub and Bitbucket, unmatched.** When the reader has no host login, or the source is `bitbucket`, which states no assignee, the answer is every ticket, as today, and a notice that names the reason, for example *The board knows no host login for you — showing every ticket.* A Bitbucket inbox never shows empty because its tickets carry no assignee.
- **Jira, unmatched (D23).** When no `jira:` spelling names the reader's handle, the answer is the `inbox` rows, with no notice and no count. `inbox` is today's `assignee = currentUser()` read, or `PLOT_JIRA_JQL` where an adopter sets it (`plot-host.sh:4684`): before `feature/the-lanes-read-the-whole-jira-project` it is absent and the open rows are that read; after it the index holds it as `inbox`. So no Jira inbox widens at upgrade.
- **Jira, declared but matching nothing.** A reader may declare `jira:<email>` on an instance that returns only `accountId`. When the reader's `jira:` spellings name no assignee of the `inbox` rows and those rows are not empty, the answer is the `inbox` rows, as for an unmatched reader, and a notice names the values Jira returns, for example *Jira names your tickets' assignee `5b10ac8d82e05b22cc7d4ef5`; `People` declares `jira:jan.wloka@quatico.com` for jwloka — add `jira:5b10ac8d82e05b22cc7d4ef5` to match.* So a reader finds the spelling on the board, no Jira inbox narrows to empty, and no host call is added. Open Point below.
- ***Only my work*** applies `isMine` over the ticket arm on the rows the client renders, with `waiting.source`. In the default view it changes nothing; with *show every ticket* on it hides the tickets assigned to somebody else and keeps the unassigned ones.
- **One owner for the Jira inbox choice.** `waitingTickets` in this plan's `feature/the-delivery-tab-filters-by-sprint-and-owner` chooses which rows *Waiting on you* shows on every source, the `inbox` rows included. `feature/the-lanes-read-the-whole-jira-project` writes the `inbox` rows into the index and passes them to the board's read, and chooses nothing; its project-wide read waits on this slice, so the drop of `assignee = currentUser()` lands only after the filter exists (`DESIGN-phases.md` §5 *Scope*).

**Every view state is a domain property.** The tab, the Planning columns, which tickets *Waiting on you* shows, how many it hides and why, and whose a ticket is come from `boardTabOf`, `planningColumns`, `waitingTickets`, `ticketSourceOf` and `ticketOwnership` in `@plot-pm/domain`, with unit tests. The *Active sprint* selection and its disabled state and the verification row's entries come from the payload through `activeSprintButton` and `verificationRows`, board helpers with unit tests. The components render them.

### Cost

- **Host and tracker calls.** Zero new calls per scan: the assignee rides the existing `issue-list` reads, and the declared-but-unmatched Jira notice reads the `inbox` rows the pass already holds. On GitHub the nested `assignees` connection keeps a 100-issue page at about 1 GraphQL point.
- **Payload.** One login or email per assignee per ticket. `waiting` adds at most 50 rows to `/api/fleet` beside today's 50 `issues`; the index grows by about 25 KB at 1000 rows. `releasedOnly` adds one boolean per column to `/api/board`; *Show released* adds nothing, because Released plans already ride it.
- **The index version.** None: `assignees` decodes absent as `[]`.
- **Server work.** `waitingTickets` runs once per issue read over at most 1000 rows in memory.
- **The waits.** *Waiting on you* is the last wave and waits on `feature/the-index-holds-closed-issues`, the fourth slice of `tickets-and-stories-have-lanes`, which D29 holds until the lanes spike's Acceptance holds in CI. The tabs, *Active sprint* and the verification row wait on nothing outside this plan.
- **Shell.** The assignee slice adds no net shell lines. The rename slice edits two comments in `skills/plot/scripts/plot-fleet-scan.sh` (`:1883`, `:4152`) and adds no line; every other slice touches no shell.
- **Tests and docs of the rename.** 47 files under `packages/board/test` open the old `agents` value; they keep it and exercise the redirect. Three files click a tab button by its old label and change: `agents-tab.browser.test.ts`, `mine-filter.browser.test.ts`, `unreachable-overlay.browser.test.ts`. `grep -rlE "Agents tab|Plans tab|Stories tab|tab=(agents|plans|stories|board)"` over `skills`, `docs`, `README.md` and `packages/board/src`, outside `docs/plans` and `docs/stories`, names 24 files. The rename changes the 17 current ones: 16 under `packages/board/src` and `plot-fleet-scan.sh`. The 7 dated release lists and sprint files keep their words.
- **Browser tests.** Seven new cases in two existing files: the tab redirect, *Show released*, the *Active sprint* click, the verification row and the two *Waiting on you* sentences in `agents-tab.browser.test.ts`, and *Only my work* over tickets in `mine-filter.browser.test.ts`. No new browser file starts a board server.
