# Design work proves the path

> A plan in Design names one slice as its spike, a person approves the spike alone, and after its merge a person confirms the remaining slices and approves the plan. A tracer-bullet plan proves a path for other plans, stays in the Design column, reads `proven` when its slices merge, and ships with its first dependent.

## Status

- **State:** Draft
- **Type:** feature
- **Story:** plot-works-in-five-phases
- **Review:** pr
- **Impl:** own branches
- **Rounds:** 3

## Changelog

- A plan in Design can name one slice as its spike with a `<!-- spike -->` annotation on its branch line. A person approves the spike alone, and the spike can be dispatched while the rest of the plan waits.
- After the spike merges, a person confirms or rewrites the remaining slices and approves the plan into Development. Approval refuses a plan whose spike has not merged.
- The approval of a plan in Design always needs a named person, on every route and whatever its `Review:` field says. A `Review: ballot` plan keeps its tally.
- A plan with a `Tracer: yes` line in its `## Status` block is a tracer-bullet plan, and its `Type:` keeps `feature`, `bug`, `docs` or `infra`. It stays in the Design column, reads `proven` when all its slices merge, and is delivered and released with the first plan that waits on it. A person may deliver a tracer plan with no dependent alone; it stays in the Design column until it is released.
- A release cut while a plan is in Design ships its merged spike's changeset, and the release cross-check lists it as a spike of a Design plan.
- `plot-dispatch.sh` asks the domain whether a plan may be dispatched, in place of its own copy of the phase and `Impl:` checks.

## Motivation

D3, D4 and D5 in [the story](../stories/plot-works-in-five-phases/STORY-plot-works-in-five-phases.md) make Design the phase where a plan proves its path before its slices are confirmed. Measured on this branch at `6954a7ed1`, 2026-10-12, and rechecked at `5b9e68ce9`:

- Five places refuse a slice of a plan that is not `approved`. `sliceVerdict` answers `unapproved` (`packages/domain/src/rules/eligible.ts:43`, `:92-108`). The dispatch workflow's `plangate` refuses `design` with `state-wrong` (`workflows/dispatch.ts:527-546`). The shell copy in `plot-dispatch.sh:2957-2978` refuses it in its `*)` arm. The brief route refuses it with `state-too-early` (`workflows/implement.ts:131-138`), and dispatch refuses a branch with no brief (`dispatch.ts:470`). The commit-time `phaseGateRefusal` refuses an implementation commit while the plan is `design` (`rules/phase-gate.ts:185-192`), but only on a branch whose slug names the plan file (`packages/board/src/server/entry/gate.ts:336-345`), so a spike branch with another name passes it today.
- The approval workflow takes its channel from the plan's `Review:` field (`workflows/approve.ts:227`). For a `Review: pr` plan whose plan PR has merged, it skips the merge (`:252`), names no reviewer and records `plan-PR #N merged` (`:270-274`). The unattended refusal in the bundle applies only to `Review: in-session` (`packages/board/src/server/entry/approve.ts:480`, `:527`), and so does the 409 of `POST /api/approve` (`packages/board/src/server/approve.ts:306`).
- A spike reading exists on the board already. `isSpikeSlice` (`packages/board/src/contract/schema.ts:1488-1521`) reads a slice named `Spike` or `Tracer` as a spike for the amber badge. `/plot-approve` step 2b (`skills/plot-approve/SKILL.md:138-157`) and `skills/plot/SKILL.md:282-298` describe a `### Tracer` slice that runs on the `idea/<slug>` branch before a Draft plan is approved. Neither makes a slice dispatchable.
- `plot-plan-meta.sh:448` `norm_type` reads one of `feature`, `bug`, `docs` and `infra`, and answers `""` for any other word. No TypeScript parser reads a plan's branch annotations: `deferred:` and `waits:` reach TypeScript only as `plot-plan-meta.sh` JSON (`waves[].branches[]`), through `PlanMetaSchema` (`packages/board/src/contract/schema.ts:63`).
- The scan builds a `P` row (`file`, `phase`, `type`) and `W` rows from plan-meta JSON (`plot-fleet-scan.sh:3110`, `:3140-3144`), parses the `P` row as two tokens (`:3151-3158`), and hands `sliceVerdict` a three-column line, `outstanding<TAB>phase<TAB>states` (`:4561`), parsed by `slicesFrom` (`packages/board/src/server/entry/verdicts.ts:63-92`). The pulse `PlanSchema` (`packages/domain/src/entities/fleet.ts:695-713`) carries `file`, `phase` and `slices`.
- The state gate reads the `State:` line only, and passes a commit that removes a line (`rules/state-transition.ts:121-141`). The controller gate classifies five script names (`GATED`, `rules/ci-suite.ts:233-239`), and `ControllerAction` names four actions (`rules/controller-gate.ts:11`). With no board, `plot-state-receipt.sh --unowned-action` maps four action words (`_action_of`, `:173-180`).
- `scripts/check-shell-lines.sh` refuses a change that grows the shipped shell under `skills/` against its merge base. It stores no allowance (`:15-19`), so each slice pays for its own shell lines in the same change.
- The `waits:` annotation (`docs/plans/2026-09-01-a-slice-can-wait-on-another-plan.md`) holds a slice until a named branch of another plan merges. It needs no change for a tracer plan's dependents.

## Design

### Approach

The design spec is [DESIGN-phases.md](../stories/plot-works-in-five-phases/DESIGN-phases.md) §4.

**The marker.** `<!-- spike -->` on a branch line is the one marker that lets a slice run before its plan is approved. `plot-plan-meta.sh` reads it in both slice dialects (list items and `### Name (Branch: …)` headings, `plot-plan-meta.sh:391-392`) and reports `waves[].branches[].spike`, present and `true` only on an annotated branch, in the same way it reads `deferred:` and `waits:`. It also reports `spike_approved_raw` and `tracer`, true when the `## Status` block holds `Tracer: yes` (D15). `norm_type` (`plot-plan-meta.sh:448`) does not change, because the type word stays. `PlanMetaSchema` gains the three fields. The domain rule `spikeOf(slices)` takes the parsed slices as a value and answers the spike branch and its slice index, or none: a plan with more than one annotated branch has no spike, and nothing refuses the file.

**The existing spike reading stays a display rule.** `isSpikeSlice` answers *what kind of outcome does this slice have*, from its name, for the badge. `spikeOf` answers *which branch may run before approval*, from the annotation, for the gates. The board's spike badge shows when either answers yes. A slice named `Spike` or `Tracer` with no annotation keeps the badge and opens no gate. The `### Tracer` heading keeps its current meaning for a Draft plan, and `/plot-approve` step 2b keeps it. For a plan in Design, step 2b reads the spike annotation instead, and `skills/plot/SKILL.md` *tracer-bullets* names both paths and which phase each serves. Its sentence at `:284`, that `/plot-approve` "parses only `### Implementation` and skips tracer branches", describes no code: the slice rewrites it as what step 2b asks the agent to do.

**Where the spike sits.** The spike branch is the only branch under the first slice heading. `approveSpike` refuses `spike-not-first` otherwise, and `spike-none` for a plan with no annotated branch or more than one. The fold in `sliceVerdicts` (`rules/eligible.ts:110-130`) then needs no exemption: the spike slice reads `complete` once it merges (`:96`), and every later slice reads `unapproved` until the plan is approved.

**The readings reach the scan.** The scan's `P` row gains three readings from plan-meta: `tracer` (`1` or `0`), the indexes of the slices that hold an annotated branch (comma-separated, or `-`), and `spike_approved` (`1` when `spike_approved_raw` is not empty). The `P` parse at `:3151-3158` becomes one `IFS=$'\t' read` of five fields. The verdict line gains two columns, `spike` (`1` when this slice's index is among the annotated ones) and `spike_approved`, and `slicesFrom` parses five columns. Each column is a reading: `sliceVerdicts` applies `spikeOf` and the phase to them, so the decision stays in the rule. The pulse `PlanSchema` gains `tracer` from the same `P` row. Two plans read `tracer`: this plan's tracer slices, and the verification run of `the-fleet-verifies-what-it-delivered`, which starts no run for a tracer plan and whose `feature/the-fleet-verifies-a-delivered-plan` waits on this slice. All three readings ship in the markers slice, because that slice pays for their shell lines (*The shell line ratchet*).

**`plot-dispatch.sh` asks the domain.** Its phase `case` (`:2957-2978`, 22 lines) and its `Impl:` `case` (`:2983-3004`, 22 lines) duplicate `plangate`, which decides both, and `eligible.ts:36-42` declares the first duplicate. Both cases and the two `sed` lines that feed them (`:2954-2955`) leave, and the script pipes the plan-meta JSON it already holds to a new `plangate` question of `board/plot-ask.mjs`, which prints the domain's refusal and exits non-zero. This is the cost rule of *A Shell Script Asks The Domain*: dispatch runs once per operator command. The contract tests that assert the shell's refusal words read the domain's words. The controller gate refuses a shell command line that names `plot-dispatch.sh`, so the worker edits it with the Edit tool.

**Approving the spike.** A new transition `approveSpike(plan, input)` accepts a `design` plan with exactly one spike, in first position, and no `Spike approved:` record. It needs `--who` from `People` on every review channel, because D4 makes the spike approval a person's act. It writes `- **Spike approved:** <date>, <who>, <channel>`. A workflow of the same name holds the host writes: for a `Review: pr` plan with an open plan PR it writes `pr-ready` and a `pr-merge` pinned with `--match-head` to the head SHA the person reviewed, as the approve bundle does (`entry/approve.ts:586`). It then writes the record as a booking commit on the default branch, as `approve` does (`workflows/approve.ts:304-311`), and clears the spike branch's `.plot/hold` entry.

**The spike approval is a mode of the approve controller.** `board/plot-approve.mjs --spike <slug> --who <handle>` runs `approveSpike`, and `POST /api/approve` with `"spike": true` runs that mode through the script arm only, never through a configured `Approve command`. The action is `approve`: the controller receipt the endpoint records (`server/approve.ts:352`) and the board-free route both stay as they are, so `GATED`, `ControllerAction` and `_action_of` do not change, no second `plot-approve*` name appears for the glob rule at `rules/ci-suite.ts:284-292`, and the slice adds no shell. With no board, the route is `plot-state-receipt.sh --unowned-action approve <slug> "<reason>"` and then the bundle with `--spike`, as for an approval. The bundle refuses under `PLOT_UNATTENDED=1`. An *Approve spike* card button calls the endpoint. `CLAUDE.md` *The Master Agent Uses The Controllers* (`:499`) gains one clause for `--spike`, and `./scripts/check-agents-md.sh --write` regenerates `AGENTS.md`.

**Gating the spike approval.** The `Spike approved:` record opens dispatch, so a hand edit must not write it, change it or remove it. `rules/state-transition.ts` gains `spikeApprovalOf(text)` and a transition of the record, and `plot-state-gate.sh` refuses a commit that adds, changes or removes the record without a receipt. The bundle writes the receipt through the TypeScript `recordStateReceipt` (`server/entry/ladder.ts:64`) under the key `<plan path>#Spike approved`, with the record's value. A `State:` receipt is keyed on the bare path, so the gate tells the two apart and one never spends the other. The refusal for a removal names no command, because no command removes the record; a plan dropped after its spike keeps it (D17). `CLAUDE.md`'s state-gate paragraph names the record. Manifesto Principle 2 (`skills/plot/MANIFESTO.md:21-23`) gains one sentence: a recorded spike approval licenses implementation of its one branch.

**Dispatching the spike.** Each of the five places above admits the spike branch of a `design` plan with a `Spike approved:` record, and nothing else of that plan:

- `sliceVerdicts` reads the two new columns and admits the slice that `spikeOf` names, for a `design` plan whose `spike_approved` reads `1`.
- `plangate` passes such a plan, and the dispatch workflow refuses `spike-only` for any other branch of it. `plot-dispatch.sh` follows with no change, because it asks `plangate` since the markers slice.
- `implement` admits the spike branch of such a plan and writes its brief. A non-spike branch still refuses `state-too-early`.
- `phaseGateRefusal` takes the spike branch and the record as readings and passes a commit on the spike branch.

One test per place proves it with a spike branch and a non-spike branch of one Design plan, and one end-to-end test proves the path (*Acceptance*). Automatic dispatch keeps its `approved` filter; `the-fleet-writes-the-design` widens it.

**Confirming the slices.** `approve` (`transitions/plan.ts:258`) refuses a `design` plan whose spike has not merged, with the refusal `spike-unmerged` that names both ways forward: wait for the merge, or remove the `spike` annotation. The phase flip is a destructive decision, so the reading is the host's `mergedAt` for the spike branch's PR, read through the host port's `prMerged(branch)` (`ports/host.ts:174`) that the approve bundle already holds, and passed as a precondition; a merge subject and ancestry are not read. The approval writes the usual `Approved:` record and no second record: `Approved:` with its date, person and channel, beside the `Spike approved:` record, already says that a person confirmed the slices after the spike.

**The approval of a Design plan is a person's act (D10, D13).** The domain rule `approvalArm({ phase, review })` answers `person`, `pr` or `ballot`, and it keys on the phase, not on a record: a `design` plan answers `person` for `Review: pr`, `in-session`, `NONE` and an empty field. A `ballot` plan answers `ballot` in every phase, because the tally is already the act of named people, and `approve` keeps refusing it with `review-human`. On the `person` arm `--who` must name a handle in `People`, the record names channel `in-session`, and an open plan PR still merges pinned to its head, so a Design plan whose PR never merged reaches the default branch. The `State:` line is the key because the state gate already refuses a hand edit of it, and a plan whose `State:` line is removed refuses `state-unreadable` (`workflows/approve.ts:169-174`). Removing the `Spike approved:` record or the annotation therefore leaves the arm unchanged, and so does a person who merges a spec commit on the plan PR (`the-fleet-writes-the-design`). Three readers ask the one rule: `workflows/approve.ts` and `transitions/plan.ts`; the bundle's `inSession` reading (`entry/approve.ts:480`), so the unattended refusal at `:527` applies; and the 409 check of `POST /api/approve` (`server/approve.ts:306`), which reads the plan's phase and review and answers 409 on the agent arm for every `person` answer. `/plot-approve`'s unattended instruction for `Review: pr` (`skills/plot-approve/SKILL.md:122-124`) names the exception. This lands in the second slice, before any slice merges a plan PR at a spike approval.

**The slice rewrite reaches the approval.** The approval commits in a booking worktree cut from `origin/<main>` and stages only the plan file, `.plot/hold` and the sprint file (`workflows/approve.ts:328-333`). The person's rewrite of the remaining slices therefore lands on the default branch before the approval, through any route the repository allows for a plan-only change; the phase gate passes a plan-only commit (`phase-gate.ts:187`). The approval refuses `slices-unpushed` when the plan file in the person's checkout differs from the copy on `origin/<main>`.

**Where the plan lives while its spike runs.** The fleet reads plans on the default branch. For a `Review: pr` plan, approving the spike merges the plan PR with `State: Design` and the `Spike approved:` record, so the fleet can read the spike, and a person records the later approval in-session (D13).

**What the spike leaves on the default branch.** The spike is real code that lands while the plan is in Design (D4). The reconcile scan does not read it as drift: *Merged-but-not-delivered* reads `approved` plans only (`plot-reconcile-scan.sh:1135`), and the symlink check has no `design` arm (`:1035`). A test keeps that true. A rewrite of the remaining slices keeps the spike slice, which reads `complete`. The spike PR carries a changeset like any slice. A release cut in this window ships it, and `/plot-release` step 3 lists a changeset whose plan is in Design with a merged spike as a spike of a Design plan, not as an entry with no delivered plan (D16). When the plan is dropped after the spike merged, the spike's code stays on the default branch, and a revert is a slice of a new plan (D17); no command writes `Rejected` or `Superseded` today (`rules/state-transition.ts:102-103`), and this plan adds none.

**Where a Design plan's other branches sit on the board.** `draftPlacement` (`rules/draft-placement.ts:45`) places only a `draft` plan's branches. A `design` plan's branches that may not run read the same as a Draft plan's: `draftPlacement` answers for `design` too, except the branch that `spikeOf` names once the `Spike approved:` record exists, which takes the arm of a dispatchable branch.

**The tracer plan.** `phaseOfPlan` reads a plan with `Tracer: yes` in the phase that holds `plan:design`, in every state but `released`. `PlanReadings` (`rules/phase.ts:113-124`) gains `tracer`, and its builders pass it, the board wrapper `planStatus` (`server/board.ts:1085-1097`) among them. `planStatus` (`rules/phase.ts:147`) answers a new value, `proven`, for an approved tracer plan whose every non-deferred slice has merged. `proven` replaces `deliverable` for a tracer plan, so the auto-bump does not move it to Testing. Both `PlanStatusSchema` copies gain the word (`rules/phase.ts:25-27`, `contract/schema.ts:394`), and the sprint counters count it as WIP, as they count `deliverable` (`packages/board/src/server/fleet.ts:6396`, `:6472`).

**`proven` names a plan, not a branch.** The merge-subject mode `plot-merge-subject.mjs proven` (`entry/merge-subject.ts:16`) and `packages/fleet/src/server/entry/registryd-main.ts:600` call a *branch* proven when a merge subject shows it landed. The plan status `proven` is a value of `PlanStatus` and says that every slice of a tracer plan merged. The two never meet in one reader: the first is a branch reading inside the scan, the second a plan status on the card. The brief of each slice that touches either names both.

**Delivering a tracer plan.** A dependent plan is a plan with a slice whose `waits:` names a branch of the tracer plan; `waits_on` is on the pulse branch already (`entities/fleet.ts:217`). `planAutoDeliver` (`rules/auto-deliver.ts:274-300`) skips a tracer plan on its own. When it delivers a dependent, it returns the dependent first and then every `proven` tracer plan the dependent waits on, because D5 releases a tracer plan with the first dependent that ships. It also returns a `proven` tracer plan that a `delivered` dependent waits on, so a tracer delivery that failed after its dependent's is retried on the next tick and automatic delivery never delivers a tracer plan before a dependent. Each returned plan runs the existing single-plan `deliver` workflow and bundle once (`workflows/deliver.ts:139`, `entry/deliver.ts:368`), with its own guardrails, its own state receipt and its own booking commit; neither changes shape. Delivery reads each tracer slice's merge from the host's `mergedAt`, as it does for the dependent. A person may deliver a proven tracer plan with no dependent alone through `/plot-deliver` (D14), and `phaseOfPlan` keeps it in the Design column until it is released.

**Every view state is a domain property.** The spike state of a card (*spike not approved*, *spike approved*, *spike merged, confirm the slices*), the `proven` status and the button each card shows come from `spikeOf`, the `Spike approved:` record, the spike branch's merge reading and `planStatus`, with unit tests. The board renders them, and one browser test proves each badge shows.

**The shell line ratchet.** `scripts/check-shell-lines.sh pr` compares each slice with its own merge base and grants nothing, so room freed by an earlier slice is gone for the next. Every shell line this plan adds is in the markers slice, and that slice removes more than it adds.
